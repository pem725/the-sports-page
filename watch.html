How the stakes number works

Stakes = how close the game is to a coin flip × how much the two teams matter. 100 would be a true toss-up between two teams whose seasons both hinge on it.

Closeness comes from the betting market: the moneyline with the bookmaker's cut removed, or the spread when there is no moneyline. A 50/50 game scores 1; a 90/10 game scores 0.2.

How much a team matters. College: its current ranking, which after the committee's first release is the playoff ranking. The top four (byes) count most, then the rest of the top 12, then the teams just outside it. Twelve teams make the field, but conference champions get automatic bids, so "top 12" is a guide, not the field itself. Pro: a team counts most when its playoff chance on our Odds Board is near 50%, and almost nothing when it is near 0% or 100%. A win cannot change much for a team that is already in, or already out.

What it does not know: injuries the line has not priced, rivalries, or how good the television will be. The line and the kickoff come live from ESPN's feed each time you open this page. The short notes under each game are written by the paper where we have something to say, and generated from the rankings and the line where we do not.
