D. WHERE THE TWO DISAGREE
   Given a stoppage, sports history says it reaches spring training 50% of the time; BLS says 8.6%.
   Sports history says it cancels games 40%; BLS says 5.4%.  It passes 232 days: sports 21%, BLS 2.5%.
   The ordinary American strike is short (BLS median 9 days) because the cost lands on both sides
   at once.  A sports lockout begun in December costs almost nothing until games are at stake: no
   salaries are paid in the offseason and little revenue is lost.  So the sports clock that matters
   is days to the first cancelled game, and the sports rows run long -- 18 of 22 sit above the BLS
   median.  BLS is the wrong base rate for the first ~100 days of an offseason lockout.  It is a fair
   warning for what follows: once a stoppage is long, it tends to stay long (BLS chance of ending
   within 30 days: 79% for a new stoppage, 26% for one already 115 days old).
   Sports chain (system-change stoppages): past 115 given past 75 = 4/5; past 232 given past 115 = 2/4.
   BLS chain: 63% and 46%.  Both say the same thing: the spring-training deadline is the fork.
