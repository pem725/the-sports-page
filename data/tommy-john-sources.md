# Tommy John / UCL reconstruction — what is actually sourced

Assembled 2026-08-30 for the success-ladder piece. Every figure here traces to a
named study. The ChatGPT ladder graphic that started this carries ranges with no
citations at all; none of its numbers are used unless they also appear below.

## The ladder — return rates in professional baseball

**Meta F, Meister K, Griffith TB, et al. "Epidemiology of Elbow Medial Ulnar
Collateral Ligament Surgeries in Major and Minor League Baseball Pitchers: A
Descriptive Study of 2281 Cases." Orthop J Sports Med. 2025;13(7):23259671251352199.**

- 2,281 pitchers, 2010–2023
- MLB 380 (16.6%), MiLB 1,907 (83.4%)
- Primary 2,093 (91.5%), revision 194 (8.5%)
- **Return to play, any level: 79.1%** (mean 470 ± 135 days)
- **Return to same level: 73.5%** (mean 499 ± 153 days)
- Revision RTP 71.1% vs primary 79.8%

As natural frequencies, out of 1,000 professional pitchers having the operation:

| | count |
|---|---|
| return to the same level | **735** |
| return, but at a lower level | **56** |
| never pitch competitively again | **209** |

## The ladder — spread across all levels

**Hones KM, et al. "Variable Return to Play and Sport Performance After Elbow
Ulnar Collateral Ligament Reconstruction in Baseball Players: A Systematic
Review." Arthroscopy. 2024;40(7):1997–2006.**

- 25 studies, ~2,100 elbows
- Primary RTP **57–100%**; return to same level **61–95%**
- High school and college: RTP **73–85%**, same level **55–74%**
- Revision: RTP **67–98%**, same level **42–78%**

**Buchanan et al. "Current Definitions of Return to Play After Medial UCL
Injuries and Surgery in Professional Baseball Players Prohibit Cross-Study
Comparison: A Systematic Review." Arthroscopy. 2025.** — the title is the
finding, and it is the strongest support for Sean's thesis.

## Incidence — who actually gets it

This is where the question mostly cannot be answered, and that is the story.

**Division I college is the only level with a real denominator.**

**Pescatore SM, DeShazo SJ, Weiss WM. "Frequency of Tommy John Surgery in NCAA
Division I College Pitchers Versus Weather Conditions." Orthop J Sports Med.
2025.** — 320 reconstructions against a population of **27,959** D1 pitchers,
2015-16 through 2021-22.

- overall 320 / 27,959 = **1.14%**, about **11 in every 1,000**
- warm-weather states 0.0156 per pitcher (158 of 10,139)
- cold-weather states 0.0091 per pitcher (162 of 17,820), P = .00011

**CAVEAT, and it is a large one.** From the text accessible to us we could not
establish whether 27,959 counts *pitchers* or *pitcher-seasons*. D1 carries
roughly 5,000 pitchers a season, so across seven seasons the figure looks like
pitcher-seasons. If it is seasons, 11 per 1,000 is an annual risk and a four-year
career risk is roughly four times that. If it is unique players, 11 per 1,000 is
already the career figure. **The same numerator, two reference classes, a
four-fold difference in what a parent should worry about.** Resolve against the
full text before publishing a career-risk number.

**Professional baseball has numerators but no denominator.** Meta et al. state
this outright: no population at risk, no pitcher-seasons, no incidence per 100.
Counts of surgeries are known precisely; the risk per pitcher is not published.

**Camp CL, Conte S, D'Angelo J, Fealy SA. "Epidemiology of ulnar collateral
ligament reconstruction in Major and Minor League Baseball pitchers:
comprehensive report of 1429 cases." J Shoulder Elbow Surg. 2018.** — 1,429
reconstructions 1974–2016: MLB 490 (34.3%), MiLB 939 (65.7%); 1,334 primary
(93.4%), 95 revision (6.7%).

**High school: a share, not a rate.** Erickson et al. report that 15–19-year-olds
accounted for **56.7%** of all UCL reconstructions performed in the United States
between 2007 and 2011, rising about 9.12% a year. Overall US incidence averaged
3.96 per 100,000 people per year.

Read that carefully. It says teenagers are the majority of *patients*. It says
nothing about a teenager's *risk*, because the denominator is the whole US
population, not the population of high school pitchers. A share of cases and a
rate per player are different quantities, and this figure is routinely quoted as
though it were the second one.

**Junior college: no usable data found.** We looked. If someone has a JuCo
denominator, we would like to see it.

## The honest summary

| level | numerator | denominator | can we state a risk? |
|---|---|---|---|
| High school | partial (share of US cases) | none | **no** |
| Junior college | none found | none | **no** |
| NCAA Division I | 320 | 27,959 | **yes**, with the reference-class caveat |
| Minor leagues | 1,907 (2010–23) | not published | **no** |
| Major leagues | 380 (2010–23) | not published | **no** |

Four of five rungs on the ladder have no denominator. Any graphic that prints a
percentage for all five is inventing four of them.
