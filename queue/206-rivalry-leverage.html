Sixty Games This Week. The Big One Isn’t on Saturday.

Lose to Michigan and Ohio State’s playoff chance falls from 62 in 100 to 3. But Ohio State wins that game 88 times in 100, and so does Alabama in the Iron Bowl. Nebraska at Iowa, on Friday, carries as much, and it’s close to a coin flip. That makes it the game most likely to change the 12-team field.

The question: which game of Rivalry Week will move the College Football Playoff the most? And is it the one you’ve already circled?

The answer: Nebraska at Iowa, on Friday. If Iowa wins, it makes the 12-team field in 62 of every 100 seasons we played out. If it loses, 1 in 100. And Iowa wins only 57 of 100. There are 63 major-college games from Thursday to Saturday. No other one puts that much on the table with odds that close.

Here’s the surprise: you’d expect it to be Michigan at Ohio State, or the Iron Bowl. Those carry nearly as much. Ohio State drops from 62 in 100 to 3 with a loss; Alabama drops from nearly a sure thing to 43 in 100. But Ohio State and Alabama each win 88 of 100. A huge stake on a lopsided game usually pays the favorite and changes nothing.

What you get: a way to rank any weekend by what it will actually change, and a reason to watch Black Friday in Iowa City.

The stake, and the coin

Two numbers decide how much a game moves the playoff. Hold onto both.

The first is the stake. Picture two worldsi: one where the team wins, one where it loses. The stake is the gap between its playoff chances in those two worlds, added up for both teams. Iowa’s is 61 points. Nebraska’s is zero; it’s out either way.

The second is how close the game is to a coin flip. A stake only gets paid when the game could really go either way. Multiply the two and you get the expected move: how far the result will shift the playoff, on average, once it’s played.

Now watch what that does to the list. Iowa: stake 61, nearly even, so the game is expected to move 30 points. Ohio State: stake 60, but Michigan wins only 12 of 100, so it moves 12. Indiana carries the biggest stake of the week, 67, because losing to Purdue would sink it from 79 in 100 to 11. Purdue wins 4 games in 100. Expected move: 5.

Twelve chairs

Notice something about the top of that list: in every game, one side has nothing to play for. Nebraska, Michigan, Auburn and Purdue are out whether they win or lose. Spoilers carry most of Rivalry Week.

Notice a second thing. The field is fixed at 12, so every point Iowa gains comes out of somebody else’s chair. When Iowa wins, Oregon loses 11 points of its chance; BYU, Wake Forest and Tennessee lose about 8 each. If you root for Oregon, you’re a Nebraska fan on Friday.

The biggest game isn’t the one with the best teams. It’s the one with a lot riding on it that could really go either way.

How we got these numbers

Each game’s odds come from the two teams’ SP+ ratings, the same way our Odds Board does it. We played out the rest of the season 60,000 times, this Saturday and the conference title games included. The field follows the official 2026 rules. Five champions get in: the ACC, Big Ten, Big 12 and SEC winners, plus the best of the other leagues. Notre Dame gets in if it’s in the top 12. The rest go by ranking.

The weak link is the committee, because nobody can simulate 13 people in a room. Our stand-in starts every team where the committee put it on November 17. Then each result moves it up or down, by how much that game changes its record against its schedule. It knows nothing about injuries or head-to-head. We tested it once. Start from the November 10 ranking and add the next weekend’s real results: it named all 12 of the committee’s top 12, most within a spot. But just keeping the old order was off by about as much. One week is a thin test, so read every number here as “about,” not “exactly.”

This Saturday will reshuffle the list. If Iowa loses at Illinois, Friday’s game goes cold. We’ll run it again after the next ranking.

What to take home

Before any big weekend, ask two questions. How much changes if this game flips? And how likely is the flip? The famous game usually wins the first question and loses the second. So on Friday, while everybody saves the couch for Saturday, turn on Nebraska at Iowa. It’s the game most likely to change who plays for a title.
