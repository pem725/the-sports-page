The Best Teams Play Thanksgiving Night. The Game That Matters Kicks Off at 1.

Bills and Chiefs, 9–1 and 8–2, are both nearly in the playoffs already. Win or lose tonight, their playoff chances move about 2 points between them. Bears at Lions at 1 p.m., both 8–2, moves 21. Here is how to tell a big game from a famous one, before you sit down.

The question: which Thanksgiving game should you not miss?

The answer: the 1 p.m. game, Bears at Lions. For each team, winning instead of losing is worth about 10 points of playoff chance. The night game, Chiefs at Bills, moves about 2 points in all.

Why that is surprising: most of us rank games by the records. Tonight’s game has the best two. But a game matters by how much it changes, and you can’t change much for a team that is already in.

What you get: one question to ask about any game on any day, and a card for each game on the table.

What a game is worth

Here is the idea in one line. A game is worth the gap between what a win buys and what a loss costs. We play out the rest of the season 200,000 times, using the chances on our Odds Board. Then we split those seasons in two: the ones where a team won on Thursday, and the ones where it lost. The gap in playoff chance between the two piles is the game’s worth to that team. Add both teams and you have the swing.

Notice the shape. The two long red bars belong to teams with something to settle. The short gray ones belong to teams whose season is mostly decided: the Bills are in, and the Eagles, at 3–7, mostly are not.

The cards for the table

1:00 · Bears at Lions (CBS). Swing: 21. Both teams are 8–2. A Bears win puts their playoff chance at 96 in 100; a loss drops it to 84. The Lions go 88 or 97. Our numbers give Detroit 53 in 100; ESPN’s lean Chicago. Call it a coin flip, which is why it matters: a close game between good teams is where one result moves the most. Watch for: the halftime score. The game card shows how often a halftime lead has held up this season.

4:30 · Eagles at Cowboys (Fox). Swing: 20. This one surprises people. The records are 3–7 and 5–6. But Dallas is still alive: 26 in 100 with a win, 10 with a loss. Nearly all of the swing is Dallas’s. Watch for: a long shot’s season being decided in one afternoon.

8:20 · Chiefs at Bills (NBC). Swing: 2. The best football of the day, and the smallest stakes for the playoffs. The Bills are in, more than 99 in 100 either way. The Chiefs go 98 or 100. What is at stake is seeding, who rests the first week and who plays at home. That is real, and it is a different question from getting in. Watch for: just the football. You have earned it by then.

And if your family’s team plays Sunday, here is a twist. Two games this weekend swing more than anything on Thursday: Raiders at Browns, 34 (Cleveland carries 28 of it), and Seahawks at 49ers, 28 (Seattle carries 26). Long faces at dinner may get their turn on Sunday.

Sidebar: Do the Lions always lose on Thanksgiving?

Mostly, yes. From 1999 to 2025 they went 8–19 on Thanksgiving, 30 wins in 100. In their other games those seasons they won 41 in 100.

But look at who they were. They were the underdog in 18 of the 27 games and won 1 of those. When they were favored, 9 times, they won 7. The pregame chances expected about 12 wins. They got 8. A shortfall that big turns up by luck about 6 times in 100: unusual, not spooky.

One more myth falls. The Cowboys were never the Lions’ Thanksgiving opponent in those years; mostly it was the Packers, Bears or Vikings. Bears and Lions met 6 times on the holiday and split them, 3 and 3.

What to take home

Ask what a game changes, not who is playing. The best records and the biggest stakes are often in different games. Stakes live where a season is still undecided and the game is close to a coin flip.

A team that is already in has little left to win. That is not a knock on the Bills. It is what being 9–1 has already bought them.

And be thankful today. There is a lot to be thankful for: three games, a full table, and people who will argue with you about all of it. Pass the stuffing.

How we did it

Data. Records and the rest of the schedule from nflverse, through games of Nov. 23, checked against ESPN. Each remaining game’s chance comes from our Odds Board.

The simulation. 200,000 runs of the rest of the season. Playoffs: four division winners and three wild cards per conference, by wins, with ties broken at random; the NFL’s real tiebreakers are not modeled. Our simulated playoff chances land within 2 points of the Board’s for every team. A game’s swing is each team’s playoff chance in the runs where it won, minus the runs where it lost, added over both teams.

Uncertainty. The simulation’s own noise is under half a point on every number here. The real uncertainty is in the game chances we feed it, and the swing depends on them: a game we call a coin flip that is really 70–30 would swing less. The Lions history uses pregame chances from closing prices, as collected by nflverse; the “about 6 in 100” is the chance of 8 or fewer wins given those 27 chances.

Limits. Seeding is not modeled, which undersells the Bills–Chiefs game for anyone who cares about a first-week rest. Code: research/thanksgiving/ in our repository.
