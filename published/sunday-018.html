What We Got Right
  • Detroit trade framing (Issue #130): The editorial conclusion — trading Skubal was rational for a team that had honestly assessed its window — holds. Tigers sitting at 57-60 is exactly the kind of “we tried, we know where we are” record the model predicted.
  • Skenes skill-vs-noise (Issue #128): The core argument — that ERA is lagging noise and K/9 is the leading signal — continues to hold. Eleven strikeouts per nine innings, unchanged week over week. The regression call is still pending, but nothing has contradicted the underlying thesis.
Factual Updates Since Publication
  • Mets post-deadline: Now 51-67, rebuilding. The bullpen sell-off documented in Issue #132 was the capstone. This is not a forecasting failure — the piece was a documentary, not a prediction. The Mets are where they are.
  • NHL: The Carolina Hurricanes won the 2026 Stanley Cup. The Rangers missed the playoffs for the second consecutive year. Issue #133’s goalie-pull math remains valid regardless of the offseason context.
  • Skenes win-loss: Now 9-10 (was 9-9 at publication). The extra loss sharpens the ERA-vs-skill tension without changing the underlying model. Still watching.

Over-Reactions & Under-Reactions

What We Over-Reacted To

Two consecutive Skenes issues (Issues #128 and #129) were defensible editorially — the first graded his current performance, the second built out the methodology — but in retrospect they read as consecutive pitching issues on back-to-back days. The variety rule exists for a reason. A CFB or NHL piece between them would have served the reader better.

What We Under-Reacted To

The Mets rebuild is bigger than a single bullpen-sale piece. Going from a legitimate playoff contender to 51-67 involves decisions that span roster construction, contract strategy, and ownership direction. Issue #132 documented the symptoms. The diagnosis — how a 3.1 ERA bullpen ended up on a last-place team — deserved more space.
