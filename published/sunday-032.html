What yesterday’s college football showed

Notre Dame went to Boston College and led 31–0 at halftime. The final was 41–7. The Irish are 9–1 and move to No. 3 in the committee’s CFP rankings. The title window is open.

In Bloomington, No. 6 Indiana beat No. 15 USC 27–13. The game was tied 10–10 at halftime; Indiana scored 17 unanswered after the break. Indiana’s offense hit close to the schedule-adjusted number: 7.3 yards per play against a model that called for close to 7. USC’s offense exceeded our projection: 6.1 yards per play, not the 5 we modeled. Indiana’s defense covered the gap. For USC, whose pre-game playoff chance sat at about 22 in 100, the loss closes the window. Indiana, now 9–1, moves a step closer to the Big Ten title game.

What to watch today, and why

Ask most of the people who read this paper which game matters today and they will say Bills at Jets. It is the one we will all have on. It is also the least important game on the card.

Ask yourself two things about any game. Is it close to a coin flip? And could the result move a team in or out of January? You need both. Buffalo is in the playoffs in more than 99 of every 100 seasons we simulate. The Jets are in fewer than 1. Win or lose, neither number has room to move. Buffalo is playing for seeding now, not for a ticket in, and one November game moves a seed only a little. The rivalry is real; the stakes are thin.

Now hold Minnesota at Green Bay to the same two questions. The market makes Green Bay a 57-in-100 favorite. Our Odds Board, built on its own ratings, says 54. Both clubs sit between 55 and 75 in 100 to make the playoffs. That is a game where a single result moves a season.

What to take home, and the road ahead

What to take home: the size of a game is not the size of the crowd. Before you decide a game is big, ask two questions. Is it close? Could the result move anybody in or out? A rivalry with a settled standings table is entertainment, and that is fine. Just do not let the broadcast sell it to you as a turning point.

This week: on Tuesday, Gene’s Rule gets its test. Gene says you can tell a good team by how fast it answers a score. We checked every drive since 1999, plus baseball and hockey. The committee’s new playoff ranking lands the same night, and we will grade our college forecast against it.
