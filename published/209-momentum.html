Is Your Team Getting Hot at the Right Time?

A team that has won five straight wins its next game about one time in 100 more than its strength already says. A hot November adds nothing to a playoff run once you know the whole season. Hot teams do win in January. They were good in October.
By The Sports Page · a question from Gene and Tim

What the betting line expected from those same teams

The question: Gene and Tim put it this way: in November you have no idea what’s going to happen, because it happens in December, when a team just clicks. So does a hot November make a hot January? Is there such a thing as momentum?

The answer: we checked four sports and more than 200,000 times a team took the field. A team that has won five straight wins its next game about one more time in 100 than its strength predicts. In the playoffs, a hot finish adds nothing you can measure once you know the full season.

Here’s the surprise: hot teams really do win more in January. It just isn’t momentum. A streak is a team telling you how good it is. What you get: a way to tell a hot team from a good one, and the one number to check before you believe in either.

How we tested it

The trap is that teams on streaks are usually better teams. Compare them to .500 and you’ll find momentum everywhere. So we compared each team to what was expected of it before the game: the betting line in football, and in baseball and hockey, both clubs’ records with that game left out. Then we asked whether the streak added anything on top.

Read the chart from the dashed line. A dot on it means the streak told you nothing the strength didn’t. Every dot sits within about two wins of the line, and every bar crosses it; baseball’s only just. A 5-game streak buys about one extra win in 100. Teams on streaks do win the next one more often, because they’re good teams. The streak itself adds almost nothing.

“But the hot teams win in January!”

They do. Since 1999, NFL playoff teams with the best last five games won 61 of every 100 playoff games. The coldest won 41. That’s a gap of 20 wins per 100 games, and it’s the thing everybody remembers. Now look at the betting line for those same games: it expected 57 and 44. The market had already seen 13 of those 20 coming; the 7 left over is within the noise.

Of the last 27 Super Bowl champions, 13 finished the season among the hottest quarter of playoff teams. How good they were all year predicts about 11. College football says the same thing: teams with the hottest November won 16 more bowl and playoff games per 100 than the coldest, and most of it, 11 of the 16, was already in the line.

Notice what that means. A team that “clicks in December” was usually good in October, and the numbers that said so were out there the whole time.

A streak isn’t a force. It’s a team telling you how good it is.

— The Sports Page

So why does it feel real?

Because sometimes the hot team wins it all, and that’s the season you remember. In 2026 the Milwaukee Brewers outscored opponents by 1.8 runs a game over their last 30, the best of any playoff club, and won the World Series. Across the 28 seasons before it, a hot September added nothing to October once you knew the whole season.

Researchers have argued about this since 1985, when a famous study called the basketball “hot hand” an illusion. In 2018 two economists found a math trap in that test, and a small real hot hand behind it. Teams look the same way: a little real, and a lot smaller than it feels.

When do you see it? Right now

In the NFL, Buffalo, Cincinnati, Detroit and Denver have each won five straight, and Chicago four. Check the margin before you believe it: Denver is 7–2 and has outscored opponents by only 12 points all season. That’s a good record on a thin team. In college, Utah and UCLA have won 10 straight. In the NHL, Edmonton has won six in a row; Toronto has lost seven.

The limits, plainly. An NFL team’s last five games include Week 18, when some teams rest starters. Baseball and hockey have no betting lines in our data, so their yardstick is rougher. And college football’s playoff alone is only 50 games, too few to settle anything by itself.

What to take home

When your team gets hot, enjoy it. Then check the margin, not the streak. A streak is your team showing you how good it is, and the betting line hears it the same day you do. If you want to know who’ll be hot in January, look at who’s been good since September.
