Same approach, same results.

One story

A thousand cases

Base rates ignored.
Anecdotes win.
Numbers feared.

John Allen Paulos, Innumeracy (1988)

If we keep doing what we have been doing, we will keep getting what we have been getting. And we know what that is. People misuse base rates. They are easily persuaded by an anecdote: one vivid story outweighs a thousand cases. And most people are, plainly, innumerate. John Allen Paulos wrote a whole shelf of books about this, starting with Innumeracy in 1988. We have all lived it. The longer I taught, the more amazed I was at how resistant people are to learning these tools. Not my students alone: all of us. Keep this point short. The room already agrees with it; you are just naming it. Underneath all of it is a distorted lens: fear or disgust or hope finds its own evidence and grows. The fix is to get a number to it before the argument, then talk about belief, then uncertainty.
