Any statistician worth their weight in spit knows that the design dictates the strength of the inference, and the measurement accelerates or slows it. Plenty of fields ignore both: run the model, hand over the results, let someone else explain them. Here is a real case from this week. The Bears traded two first-round picks for Jeffery Simmons. How many good years does he have left? If you choose comparison players by their career Pro Bowls, you only keep the ones who lasted, because the career is the filter, and you get a rosy answer: they played to 34. Choose them on what was knowable when they were 28, like Simmons now, and the answer is about two and a half starter seasons in the next four, with one in five getting one or none. Same question, same data, opposite stories. The model never changed. The design did.
