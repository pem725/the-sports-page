The university

Donald T. Campbell · The Experimenting Society

But here is what I am really doing. I am not just carrying statistics out of the university. I am digging up what a university was supposed to offer in the first place: a place where people come to make sense of things. So flip it. Make the university the water cooler. Bring everybody here, and have them learn the basics because they need them to function. Something as small as answering a few questions on your way to class. Constantly nudging people to grow what they know, out of necessity. And the basics are blocking and tackling: fractions, what the denominator is, the common fallacies. Fluent enough that it is intuitive. If I tell you it is 30 degrees Celsius outside, you should know it is hot, even as an American. Once people have that, they stop getting angry over numbers that can be settled by looking. The emotion goes where it belongs: in the stands, yelling and hoping and dreaming. That is exposure, delivered without people noticing, and we can dial it up and down and measure how we are doing. Don Campbell called that the experimenting society. This is one small version of it.
