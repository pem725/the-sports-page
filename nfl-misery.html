After Week 8, the Miami Dolphins project to 3.9 wins (80% range 2 to 6), the fewest in the league. The biggest fall from August belongs to the Los Angeles Chargers: 9.3 wins expected then, 4.4 now.

The Misery Slide

Projected wins: the average of 40,000 simulated seasons, with the range that holds 8 seasons in 10. vs Aug.: today’s projection minus the wins expected in August. Tap a row to follow a club.

How to read it

What is the question? With about seven games played, which club can expect the least from the rest of its season? Notice that this is not the same as which club is worst so far. A bad record against a hard schedule can still have an easy run left.

The solid part of each line is what happened: the club’s winning percentage after each week. The dashed part is the projection: where that percentage is expected to go, game by game, on the real remaining schedule. A flat stretch is a bye. Tap a line, or pick a club, to see its band and every remaining game.

The other view is the cumulative one. For each club it plots the chance of finishing with N wins or fewer. A curve that shoots up early on the left is a club whose season is close to settled.

How we built it. We started every club where the betting market had it the day before kickoff, then let this season’s scores move it. How far the scores are allowed to move a club depends on how wrong August usually is: we measured that from 2015–2025 at 3.8 points of strength. Then we played the rest of the season 40,000 times.

Does it work? We ran the same recipe on 2023–2025 at the same point of the season and scored the 495 games that followed. Updating on results beat the August view alone (Brier score 0.233 against 0.245; lower is better, a coin scores 0.250). But the market's own ratings at the same moment did better (0.226), and each game’s closing line, which knows about injuries we do not, scored 0.204. Read this as a fair summary of the scores, not as a better forecast than the market.
