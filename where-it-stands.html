How to read it

The big number is the season. It says how many of every 100 regular hitters that same year this one beat. It leads because it holds the rules, the ballparks and the strategy of the day fixed: everyone in it faced the same league.

The second line is all time. The same number against every regular season since the stat was kept. When the two disagree, the season was unusual: a low all-time number with a high season number means the whole league struggled that year.

The third line is the league. The typical regular that season, and where that typical season ranks among all of them.

100 is kept for the single top value; everyone else tops out at 99. Regulars means 300 or more plate appearances, scaled down for short seasons (1918, 1981, 1994, 2020), so a 50 is an ordinary everyday player, not an ordinary major leaguer. Strikeouts start in 1913, when both leagues kept them. Source: the MLB Stats API, rebuilt each winter.
