How to Watch a Football Game

You do not need to know every rule. You need to know where to look, and what the people on TV mean.

The bottom line: football is a fight over 10 yards. One team gets four tries to go 10 yards. Make it, and it gets four more. Miss it, and the other team gets the ball.

So watch the down and the yellow line on the screen. Almost everything else is detail.

Before the snapWhat to look at in the first quarter

Look at the corner of the screen first. It says something like "2nd & 7": the second try, with 7 yards still to go. That one line tells you how nervous to be.

Watch the first few plays to learn each team’s plan. Coaches often script their opening plays in advance. Does a team run the ball or throw it? Is the quarterback getting time to throw, or running for his life? You will see the plan before the announcers say it.

Then pick one player to watch for a whole drive. Not the ball. Try the quarterback, then a big lineman. You will see a different game.

Down by downWhat each try is for

1st down

The free try. The offense can run or pass, and the defense has to guess. A good gain here makes the next two easy.

2nd down

Sets up third. Short yardage left means the offense is in charge. Long yardage means trouble.

3rd down

The big one. Most drives live or die here. Lean in.

4th down

Usually a kick: a punt to push the other team back, or a field goal for 3 points. Sometimes a team goes for it, and then everyone holds their breath. See going for it.

TranslationWhat the announcers mean

“Moving the chains.”
They made a first down and got four new tries. A sideline crew holds a 10-yard chain and literally moves it. More.
“They’re in the red zone.”
Inside the last 20 yards before the end zone. Now the question is touchdown (7 points) or field goal (3). More.
“He’s got a clean pocket.”
The blockers are keeping defenders away from the quarterback, so he has time to throw. More.
“They’re going to take the points.”
On fourth down, the coach is kicking a field goal for 3 points instead of gambling on a touchdown. More.
“The two-minute drill.”
The hurry-up at the end of a half. The team throws to the sidelines and runs out of bounds to stop the clock. Watch the clock and the timeouts left. More.
“They’re bringing pressure” or “a blitz.”
The defense sent extra players after the quarterback. Big risk, big reward. More.
“Play-action.”
The quarterback faked a handoff and then threw. Watch the defenders step toward the fake. More.
“They’re in Cover 2.”
A defense with two players standing far back, each guarding half the field. See the diagram.

Your turnFive things worth talking about

  1. “Should they go for it?” On fourth down, ask the room before the coach decides. Everyone has a view, and nobody is sure. That is the fun of it.
  2. “Who’s winning up front?” Count how often the quarterback gets hit or hurried. The team winning that battle usually wins the game, even when the score says otherwise for a while.
  3. “Was that a good call?” After a yellow flag, guess the penalty before the referee announces it. Holding and pass interference are the usual suspects.
  4. “Seven or three?” Every time a team reaches the red zone, call it: touchdown or field goal? Keep a tally. It often explains the final score.
  5. “Who’s ahead at halftime?” Make a halftime call on the winner, then see whether it holds. Our game cards tell you how often the team ahead at the half wins this season.
