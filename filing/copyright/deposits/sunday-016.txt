What We Got Right
The Wins
The Mets diagnosis is holding. Issue #110 said the May acceleration was noise and the negative level was the real story. The Mets are now ~43–60, 17 games out in the NL East, and actively shopping their center fielder. Luis Robert Jr. returned from the 60-day IL and hit two home runs in his first games back — the Mets are working the market before August 3. The framework read the team correctly from the May data, and every week of results confirms it.
The Jets structural argument from Issue #119 received independent market validation before the newsletter’s ink was dry. Vegas set the Jets’ 2026 win total over/under at 5.5 — comfortably inside the “under seven wins” gravity range the piece identified. The market did not see the analysis. It arrived at the same number. That is the right kind of corroboration: a price signal matching a model’s output, arrived at separately.
The Notre Dame checklist remains intact on every input. CJ Carr’s 24-to-6 touchdown-to-interception ratio, cited in Issue #109 and verified this morning, is confirmed. The Irish are #4 in the preseason top 25. Marcus Freeman is on Dodd Trophy watch lists. The returning production rate leads the country. Issues #115 and #117 each found the same conclusion from a different starting point — which is corroboration, not repetition, as long as we are honest that both pieces point at the same flag.
Factual Update — Not a Forecasting Miss
The Trade Deadline Is August 3, Not July 31
Issue #116’s footnote stated: “The deadline is July 31 at 6 p.m. ET; no trades are permitted afterward (rule in force since 2019).” That date is wrong. The Commissioner’s Office can set the deadline anywhere from July 28 to August 3. In 2026 it landed on the last possible day:
Monday, August 3 at 6 PM ET
.
The three-day difference does not change the winner’s curse argument, the supply analysis, or the marginal-value estimate for a rental starter. But the date was wrong and this is where we correct it, without hedge. The analysis of Issue #116 stands. The footnote does not.
One related update: Tarik Skubal’s availability at the deadline is genuinely uncertain. The Tigers are in wild-card range, which suppresses their incentive to sell their best pitcher. If Detroit holds, the biggest name on the board may never reach the market — concentrating winner’s curse pressure on a thinner shelf of alternatives. The auction-theory argument still applies. The star of the auction may have changed.

Over-Reactions and Under-Reactions
Over-Reactions
Issues #115 and #117 published two days apart and both concluded that Notre Dame is the clear favorite — one through the talent-door/maturity-door lens, one through the five-team title-board lens. That is real corroboration. But publishing the reinforcement in the same week walks the line between analytical rigor and apparent repetition. If the Irish lose, two separate pieces will be wrong together; the analytical independence of those two issues is lower than it looks at a glance. The call was worth making loudly. It did not need to be made twice in four days.
Under-Reactions
Issue #119 proved the Jets are a local minimum — a franchise stuck below seven wins by something structural rather than by bad luck — and then stopped. The natural next question is: which organization actually bent its franchise gravity upward, and what did that escape look like in the data? Kansas City rebuilt its institutional mean. Philadelphia did too. The architecture for that piece was built in Issue #119 and not published. It should be. It is in the queue, somewhere.
