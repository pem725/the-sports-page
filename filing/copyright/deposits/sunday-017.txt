The Deadline Came. The Mets Sold.
The Scorecard Pays.
We called it a broken franchise — a club whose institutional gravity pulls it toward .500 regardless of payroll, regardless of July hope, regardless of what the standings imply about a bounce. The trade deadline just published our thesis in transactions.
By The Sports Page · August 2, 2026 · Sunday Edition No. 017
The 2026 MLB trade deadline closes today at 6 PM ET. By Saturday morning, the Mets had already moved A.J. Minter to Minnesota and were actively shopping Freddy Peralta, Luis Robert Jr., and Clay Holmes. A team that believed its own bounce-back narrative would buy, or at minimum hold. The Mets are selling. Issue #121 published six days ago. The front office answered in 48 hours.

    This week’s scorecard closes out three predictions, partially grades a fourth, and carries two into fall. The Rays did what the Pythagorean model said they’d do. The Dodgers did what any sane front office does when they’re already the best team in the sport and a two-time Cy Young winner becomes available. And the Mets did what Issue #121 said they would — not with their record, but with their behavior. Regression to the mean is about where you end up, not the path you take to get there.

What We Got Right
The Wins
The Mets diagnosis is the cleanest hit of the summer. Issue #110 said the negative level was the real signal. Issue #121 said the institutional mean would dominate over any statistical bounce-back. The front office confirmed it by Saturday morning: trading their left-handed reliever and actively shopping two starters and a center fielder before the deadline. That is not a team that believes in its own rebound. That is a team that knows exactly what it is.
The Rays call is the most precise quantitative grade we have published. The Pythagorean model projected a .538 second-half pace. The Rays are running .533 (8–7) since the break. That is essentially the model, running on the field. Junior Caminero’s historically anomalous production explains why the Rays are still in first place — but the overall pace dropped exactly as the surplus model predicted. Pythagorean math works, even when a twenty-three-year-old third baseman is trying to disprove it.
The Notre Dame checklist holds. CJ Carr’s 24-to-6 TD/INT ratio is confirmed. Freeman is on Dodd Trophy watch lists. The Irish are #4 in the preseason top 25. The flag planted in Issue #109 has not moved. Five weeks until we find out whether September agrees.

No Forecasting Misses — Two Things Worth Noting
On Skubal and the Winner’s Curse
Issue #116 predicted the highest bidder would overpay for the Tigers’ ace. The Dodgers won the auction with two top-100 prospects. The winner’s curse mechanism — winning bidder exceeds consensus estimate by definition — applies structurally. We are calling it PARTIALLY HIT because the dollar verdict requires October. If Skubal has multiple control years, the per-win cost changes. If the Dodgers win a third consecutive title, no one will say they overpaid. The auction theory holds. The size of the miss, if there is one, is unknown until the playoffs end.
On the Dodgers “cooling”: their second-half pace of .571 (down from .635 in the first half) is a real and modest decline. The “worth watching” framing was exactly right. What we were watching was their next move. The move was Skubal. Watch closed. They’re fine.
Over-Reactions and Under-Reactions
Over-Reactions
The Rays winning a long streak while “regressing” looks paradoxical. It is not. Regression to the mean does not mean steady decline; it means the average pulls toward the projection. The Rays went 1–5 right after the break, then ran off a nine-game win streak. Net result: 8–7, a .533 pace, exactly where the model pointed. The model does not tell you the path. It only tells you the destination.
The Mets sell-off velocity also surprised us. Issue #121 did not predict they would be shopping Freddy Peralta before August 1. It predicted institutional gravity would prevent contention. The gravity acted faster than the framing implied — which is a win, not a miss, but the speed deserves an asterisk.
Under-Reactions
We may have underweighted the winner’s curse severity for the specific Dodgers context. They are 69–41 with a +155 run differential and a full rotation. Adding a two-time Cy Young winner makes them a historically great team chasing a third straight title. From that seat, the marginal value of Skubal in October is not two wins — it might be the difference between a ring and a conference-final exit. The auction theory still applies. But the Dodgers may be the one bidder in baseball for whom the premium is justified.
Junior Caminero. He deserved his own issue this summer. A historically anomalous hot streak from a twenty-three-year-old carrying a first-place team — and we tracked the Pythagorean model instead. We watched the forest. A tree was on fire.
