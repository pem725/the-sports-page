# The four of us

The Sports Page is four people: **Tim, Sean, Patrick,** and the person who
drives it.

One person publishes it daily. The other three keep pointing it somewhere
better than it would have gone on its own. That distinction matters, and it is
the reason this file exists — the byline says "The Sports Page," and it should,
but the byline is not the whole story.

---

## What each contribution actually changed

This section records only what the project record can show. It grows as the
record grows.

### Tim

- **The velocity question.** Asked whether a radar gun tells you something an
  ERA doesn't. That became *"The Radar Gun Knows Before the ERA Does"* — an
  issue that exists because he asked. He also caught a velocity caveat that had
  to be amended into an earlier piece before it was right.
- **The subscription idea** (August 2026). Proposed that gaming sites were a
  natural client for a subscription model, and that unique access to statistical
  reasoning could be a market differentiator. The specific buyer turned out to be
  wrong — a sportsbook's margin depends on the gap between perceived and actual
  probability, and this newsletter's whole product is closing that gap — but the
  underlying instinct was right, and pointing the same idea at prediction-market
  exchanges resolves the conflict. It also lit the fire under the whole
  readership-and-growth push.

### Sean

- **Audience research.** The hover-for-a-primer mechanism, the tiered
  "kindergarten / go deeper" concept pages, and the idea that Issue #100 should
  be a quiz. The concept-primer ladder the newsletter now runs on came from this.
- **The headline dataset** (August 2026). Compiled the top two U.S. sports
  headlines for every week of a year, first as prose and then as a table.
  Preserved in `source-materials/`.
- **The insight that made it useful.** That the table's value is the
  *unscheduled but entirely predictable* headlines — college football upsets in
  Weeks 1 and 2 that reset the rankings and the playoff betting. That single
  observation is the basis of the whole 2026–27 editorial calendar: 47% of the
  sports year is exactly that kind of event, and all of it can be written in
  advance.
- **The gap.** Noted the list needed under-reported sports added. Quantified
  later: a third of the sports year happens in sports the newsletter had never
  covered once.

### The one who drives it

Writes and publishes it. Set the rules that make it what it is: one question per
issue, every number verified against three sources, no gossip, no locker-room
access, no brown-nosing. The stated goal is 500 original ideas that let ordinary
fans scratch an itch without paying someone to report rumours — *the Far Side of
sports geekdom.*

---

## The thing worth remembering

None of the three contributors above were asked for any of it. The velocity
question, the audience research, the headline dataset, the subscription idea —
all unprompted. A newsletter that publishes daily is a grind, and the reason it
keeps finding new angles is that three other people keep handing them over.

That is what "The Sports Page is four of us" means.
