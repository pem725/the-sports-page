Tonight’s question: when the best offense meets the best defense, which one gives?

The answer, from 38 games since 1970: neither does. The offense won 18, the defense 20, and the odds expected about 20 offensive wins. The No. 1 offense averaged 23 points in those games, eight under its usual 31. The No. 1 defense allowed 23, ten over its usual 13. They meet in the middle.

Why that surprises: most fans expect a winner between the irresistible force and the immovable object. Usually both sides just bend.

Section 4The Bayesian booth

A small sample can tell you something, but not everything. Each panel below starts from what is normal for the league, adds what these teams have actually done, and shows the blend. Move the sliders to see how much the evidence should move you.

1. Is Carson Wentz a downgrade?

Wentz has 48 dropbacks this season, scrambles and sacks included, and averaged +0.10 expected points each. Murray had 185 and averaged −0.05. Taken at face value, the backup has been better. But 48 plays is a small pile: one long touchdown moves the average a lot. This panel shrinks each quarterback toward the league average (+0.05) by an amount set by how much quarterbacks truly differ.

The default, 0.13, is measured: the 34 passers with 100+ dropbacks this season spread out by 0.16, and 0.10 of that is luck you would expect from 250 plays each. The leftover is talent. Per-play spread is 1.57 expected points. One more thing the average hides: Wentz has been sacked on one dropback in eight (13%), against Murray’s 8%, and Murray escaped to scramble 16 times.

2. The red-zone collision

Buffalo has scored a touchdown on 27 of 38 trips inside the 20. Minnesota has allowed one on only 8 of 21. The league rate is 63%. Both numbers are extreme, and both rest on few trips. Shrink each toward the league, then combine them on the odds scale to get tonight’s expected rate for Buffalo.

Each curve is a Beta posterior: the league rate is treated as if it had been seen on the chosen number of trips, then this season’s trips are added. To combine the two, multiply the offense’s odds by the defense’s odds and divide by the league’s odds. That is the same log5 arithmetic baseball uses for batter against pitcher.

3. Live win probability: keep this open during the game

Type the score and the time left. The pregame line (Bills by 3.5) acts as the prior. Through a normal curve that line gives Buffalo 60%, a little under the moneyline’s 64%. As the clock runs, the line counts for less and the scoreboard counts for more. Final margins in the NFL scatter with a standard deviation of about 13.5 points over a full game, shrinking with the square root of the time left.

The curve shows the Bills’ chance if the score stayed frozen at what you typed, at every point from now to the final whistle. It ignores who has the ball and timeouts, and in the last two minutes those matter. Treat anything under five minutes as rough.

Section 5Tale of the tape

Expected points added (EPA) per play measures how much each snap changed a team’s expected score, given the down, the distance and the field position. Zero is average. For an offense, higher is better; for a defense, lower. The scatter puts every team on one picture.
