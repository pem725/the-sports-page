The metric

Most sports desks pick the biggest event and hunt for numbers inside it. We do the reverse: pick the most interesting number, then explain the sport around it. Stats driven by sports, not sports driven by stats.

NEWSINESS = (GRIP ÷ 10) × (0.35 TWIST + 0.25 CLOCK + 0.15 STACK + 0.25 CARRY)

GRIP multiplies rather than adds. That is the editorial position, not a modelling convenience. A story with no number scores zero however good it sounds.

Score each 0–10. The anchors are below so two people scoring the same story land close — you should be able to do this from this page, on your phone, in five minutes.

GRIPthe number itself — multiplies everything
0 no number · 2 a number someone else published · 5 our number, one source · 8 computed from primary data, verified · 10 computed, verified three ways, with an interval and a stated limit
TWISTdistance from what a reasonable fan expects
0 confirms what everyone assumes · 3 mildly counterintuitive · 6 contradicts a reasonable prior · 9 the obvious answer is wrong and we can show why
CLOCKwhat today buys over next month
0 true forever · 3 good for a season · 6 good for a fortnight · 9 decays within days
STACKaudience, in printed pages — one page is a thousand readers
0 under a page · 3 a few hundred · 6 a ream · 9 a tree or more (a Super Bowl is about fourteen trees)
CARRYdoes the lesson survive outside sport?
0 sport only · 4 transfers to one other domain · 7 a general habit of thought · 10 changes how you read any number
“A deficit is a fact, superiority is a rate” carries. “The Rays are lucky” does not.

Answerability is a gate, not a term. If the data cannot settle it, the story is held rather than ranked — however well it scores.

Where we actually need you

  • Pick one story above and give it five numbers. That is the whole ask — one story, five scores, sent back however you like. Then tell us which number of ours you would move and why: “your TWIST on that is a 4” is worth more than a paragraph of encouragement. A metric nobody argues with is a metric nobody is using.
  • Bring questions, not topics. The best issues this year came from someone asking a question they could not settle at the table. Gene’s produced a concept primer and an issue with his name on it.
  • Tell us when a piece was boring. Nobody reports this and it is the most useful thing you can say.

The open questions

  • Readership. The target is one reader per issue published — 155 today, and roughly a thousand before we hang a shingle. We have no weekly tracker for this yet; it is the next thing to build.
  • Our own calibration. We keep telling readers a thing is 71% likely. The picks ledger will eventually say how often those happened. 0 of 15 graded so far; it needs 25+ before it can speak.
  • Publishing the rejects. This page shows what we passed over. That record should itself become an issue — readers seeing the selection, not just the selected.
  • The Stack’s anchors. The audience scale is built but its readership figures are illustrative, and every one must be verified three ways before it appears in an issue.
