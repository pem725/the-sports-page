## Method

**Step 1 — projected margin.** For each game, SP+ rating of the home side minus
the away side, plus **2.2 points of home-field advantage**. That is the margin we
expect.

**Step 2 — edge.** Subtract the market's expected margin (the negative of the
home spread). A positive number means the home side is undervalued; negative, the
away side.

**Step 3 — cross-check against a consensus, and demote on disagreement.** SP+ is
one system. Massey publishes a consensus of **39**. For every pick, we compared
which team the consensus rates higher. Where the consensus disagreed with SP+
about who is actually the better team, the edge was **halved** before ranking.

That demotion fired on six games: Georgia, Army, NC State, Illinois, Louisville,
Kansas State. Georgia is the sharpest case — SP+ makes Georgia about seven points
better than Alabama, while the consensus has **Alabama first and Georgia fifth**.
It fell from the third-highest confidence to tenth. It is the pick most likely to
be wrong, and the one the editor should feel free to overrule.

**Step 4 — rank.** Points 15 down to 1 in order of adjusted edge.

## What we are least sure of

**North Dakota State at 13 is the shakiest high number on the sheet.** They are
FCS and SP+ ratings are not reliably comparable across divisions. Both sources
like them, which is why it survived, but the edge is softer than its position
implies.

**And the ordering itself is unproven.** Across 75 graded picks this season our
confidence ranking has shown no skill at all: the top tier (13–15 points) covered
47%, the bottom tier (1–3) covered 67%, and the slope is −0.44 percentage points
per confidence point. Treat this ranking as a stated rule, not a calibrated
instrument. The reason to seal it is precisely that it has not earned trust yet.
