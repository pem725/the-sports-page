Thanksgiving: Three Close Games, and One Lead Nobody Keeps

The question: all three Thanksgiving games were decided by four points or fewer. Was that as strange as it felt?

The answer: stranger. The Bears beat the Lions 39–35. The Eagles beat the Cowboys 39–38. The Bills beat the Chiefs 13–10. Add the margins and you get 8 points across three games. We went back through 20 years of games that started out as evenly matched as these three. A day like Thursday turns up about 1 time in 75. You would expect a close day now and then; football is full of them. But three at once is another matter. The night game began in 2006, so there have been 19 Thanksgivings with three games (2020 lost one to COVID). In none of them had this happened. The closest was 2022, at 18 points.

Notice which claim we chose. “All three within one score” is far less rare, about 1 day in 7. Pick the claim before you count. If you count first and then pick, you will always find something that sounds amazing.

Dallas, at 99 in 100

Dallas came in favored by 8.5 points. That is the crowd’s prior: the line people settle on before kickoff, as they react to news and to each other. Turn it into a chance and Dallas was expected to win about 79 in 100. Late in the third quarter it led 31–10, and both in-game models we checked put its chance near 99 in 100.

Then three plays went the other way. A punt was blocked and returned for a touchdown. A 40-yard field goal sailed wide left with three and a half minutes left. And up two with 55 seconds to go, Dallas went for it on fourth down and was sacked. Philadelphia needed 11 yards to get in range, and Jake Elliott made a 59-yarder with seconds left. Eagles 39, Cowboys 38.

So was 99 in 100 a bad number? We checked it, and it was not. We took every NFL team from 2006 to 2025 that reached a 99-in-100 chance. The model expected about 21 of them to lose; 19 did. A model that is honest at 99 in 100 will be wrong about once a season, and somebody has to be that team. This year it was Dallas, on Thanksgiving, by one point.

Hold onto that the next time you hear “nobody saw this coming.” Somebody did. They saw it coming 1 time in 100, which is just what 99 to 1 means.

What we said on Thursday

Issue #241 said the 1 p.m. game, Bears at Lions, mattered most, and called it a coin flip. Chicago won it by four. We also gave each team its playoff chance after a win and after a loss. Chicago won: we said 96 in 100, and today’s board says 96. Detroit lost: we said 88, the board says 89. Dallas lost: we said 10, the board says 10. The night game was the best football of the day. It changed almost nothing, as we said it would.

What to take home

A 99-in-100 chance is a promise about a hundred games, not about this one. It works like the chance of rain. Say the forecast is 30 in 100 and you get wet. The forecast was not wrong; it rains on about 30 of every 100 days like that one. The forecast is a guess about many days. The day is just one. Judge a forecast by how its misses add up across a season, never by one night in Dallas.
