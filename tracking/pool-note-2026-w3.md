# Patrick's Pool — Week 3 — what I was thinking

Picks are locked, so here's the reasoning. The deal going forward: **once the
picks are in, everybody says what they saw.** Not to argue about it — to have it
on record so that in November we can tell whose reasons were actually doing work
and whose just sounded good. The Sports Page runs its own slate too, further down,
so the numbers have to answer for themselves as well.

Three name corrections before anyone repeats them: it's **Jon Sumrall** at
Florida, **Clark Lea** at Vanderbilt, and **Dave Doeren** at NC State. All checked
against the CollegeFootballData feed. I could not confirm the Ole Miss head coach
or the reported Clemson quarterback injury, so neither is stated as fact below.

---

## The picks, and why

**15 — South Carolina −3.5 (vs Mississippi State).** Loaded for bear, and I think
they make an unusual run at the SEC. Home, and they need a splash. Shane Beamer
has it going. Mississippi State is an odd bird — my hunch is personnel problems
coming, because they cannot pay the big money. That is a lot of points on a hunch,
and I know it.

**14 — LSU −3.5 (at Ole Miss).** Honestly this was my 15. Lane Kiffin goes back to
Oxford and shows what first-class talent looks like. I have LSU 48–20. If the
spread were 28 I might still take them.

**13 — James Madison +2.5 (at San Diego State).** My darling. Those guys learn,
they know how to win, they cover — and they're *getting* points.

**12 — Florida −2.5 (at Auburn).** I like what Sumrall is doing. And I have no
faith in Auburn; they may be a few years away.

**11 — Arizona State −5.5 (at Kansas).** Pure eye test. Fiery, genuinely good, and
once they fix some blemishes, Dillingham is a good coach. They looked far more
competitive against Texas than I expected against that level of talent. My biggest
disagreement with the paper's model, and my biggest leverage.

**10 — Texas Tech −7.5 (vs Houston).** They're building to win. Whether it's by
more than a touchdown is the part I'm unsure about. Houston is assembling real
talent. If Tech pulls away it's 28–10.

**9 — UConn −3.5 (at Southern Miss).** Both of these are dumpster fires and it's
down in Mississippi. I'm on the fence.

**8 — North Carolina +3.5 (at Clemson).** Clemson looks to be in disarray. If
North Carolina can pin their ears back I see them winning outright, in Death
Valley.

**7 — Texas State −2.5 (vs North Texas).** No enthusiasm here. Maybe I'm
undervaluing them. Home, and 0–2.

**6 — Vanderbilt −3.5 (vs NC State).** I believe in Clark Lea. I don't see the ACC
matching SEC talent, and Doeren's program is aging into mediocrity — he isn't
shocking anybody any more.

**5 — SMU +1.5 (at Louisville).** Partly because I want them 3–0, so I'll take the
points and cheer.

**4 — Old Dominion −2.5 (vs East Carolina).** Home squad. East Carolina looks like
total disarray.

**3 — South Alabama −4.5 (vs Ohio).** Ohio looks poor and the MAC may be
struggling. South Alabama has a lot of Alabama people.

**2 — Northwestern −3.5 (vs Colorado).** I didn't want to give points to a
Colorado team, but who knows how Deion's group travels. Up to Chicago. Could be a
weird game — a bellwether. Not much confidence.

**1 — Virginia Tech −3.5 (at Maryland).** *The one the model cannot see.* When
Ralph Friedgen was let go at Maryland, James Franklin had been in line and Maryland
passed him over. He went to Vanderbilt, then Penn State, and recruited against
Maryland with something close to a grudge. Now he's at Virginia Tech and has told
the Hokie faithful to flood the stadium. Saturday, 7:30, on campus — and Maryland
students do not go to football games. There's a real chance that building is
mostly orange. I'll say Virginia Tech by a touchdown, maybe more, because Franklin
is angry.

**Dropped: Wyoming / Central Michigan.**

One point on the grudge match on purpose — so I can sit back and enjoy it. And if
Maryland knocks them off? Oh, that would be *great*.

The interesting one is Arizona State–Kansas: it's the biggest stake in the
editor's slate sitting on the biggest disagreement with the model. If the numbers
have any signal at all, that's where it shows.

The Virginia Tech game is the honest test of the whole exercise. The model has no
way to represent a road crowd taking over a stadium, or a coach with a fifteen-year
grudge. That is not a flaw in the model so much as a boundary of it — and the
editor put one point on it, which is the right price for a story you want to watch
rather than believe.

**Crowd alignment:** the paper has 81 of 120 points on sides the public is *not*
on. The editor has 53. Two different theories of how you win a pool.

anything, and we are nowhere near that.

One correction on the record: the ledger had been grading these **straight up**

team beats the winning margin, so everything above is regraded against the spread.
