# Getting an idea into The Sports Page

For contributors. **Send to `ideas@thesportspage.net`.** That is the whole
delivery mechanism — you write the email, you send it. No automation, no
integration, nothing to install.

What follows is not about *sending*. It is about the **shape** of what you send.

---

## Why shape matters more than speed

A one-line idea — *"what about Tommy John surgery"* — costs an hour of digging
before anyone knows whether it is a story. The same idea arriving with its number
named and its source stated takes two minutes to triage and can be written that
week. Sean's Tommy John column ran as Issue #164 for exactly that reason: it
arrived with citations attached.

So: use whatever assistant you already have open — **Claude, ChatGPT, Codex** —
to do the formatting. Then paste and send.

## The prompt

Paste this once as a Claude Project instruction, a ChatGPT Project instruction,
or into an `AGENTS.md` if you are working in Codex. Then just talk to it.

> You help me pitch story ideas to The Sports Page, a daily statistics newspaper.
> Its rule is *stats driven by sports*, never sports driven by stats — the number
> comes first and the game explains it.
>
> When I give you an idea, reply with EXACTLY this and nothing else:
>
> **IDEA:** one sentence.
> **THE NUMBER:** the statistic at its centre. If there isn't one, write
> "no number yet" — do not invent one.
> **WHY IT'S SURPRISING:** what a reasonable fan would expect, and how the answer
> differs.
> **WHERE THE DATA IS:** the actual source. Write "unknown" if you don't know.
> Never supply a figure, ranking, standing or poll result from memory.
> **SCORE:** 0–10 each — GRIP (strength of the underlying number), TWIST
> (distance from what people expect), CLOCK (how fast it goes stale), STACK
> (audience), CARRY (does the lesson survive outside sport).
>
> Be blunt. If there is no number in it, say so in the first line. Most ideas
> should be rejected.

Then send the block to `ideas@thesportspage.net`. Add it to your contacts as
*Sports Page* so it autocompletes.

## The one rule that is not negotiable

**Ideas and framings from an assistant: yes, gratefully. Numbers: never.**

On 6 September 2026 a Sunday Edition led with *"South Carolina went from unranked
to No. 10 in the AP poll."* No such poll existed — week one ran through Labor Day,
so the first in-season poll had not been released. A search summary reconstructed
a plausible ranking from a previous season. Every surrounding fact was true: the
57–0 score, the opponent, our own preseason ranking of them. That is precisely
what let one fabricated link through, and it ran as the headline.

We published a correction and added a checker. But the durable fix is upstream:
an assistant may point you at a fact. It may never be the source of one.

If you are not certain where a number came from, write **"unknown"** and let us
chase it. That is genuinely more useful than a confident figure, because a wrong
number costs far more than a missing one.

## What happens next

Every idea is logged in `tracking/reader-questions.md` — first name only, never a
contact detail — and vetted against three questions:

1. Is there a number in it, or could there be?
2. Could it *not* be settled at the table?
3. Could a reasonable person be wrong about it?

All three yes and it earns a piece. If we have already covered it you get a link
back, usually the same day. That counts as a good outcome, not a rejection: two
of the last twenty issues came from questions somebody sent in, and the goal is
to raise that number.
