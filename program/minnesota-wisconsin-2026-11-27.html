The series, since 1892

Win, and Wisconsin keeps its one-game lead. Lose, and the series is tied for the first time since 2014.

These two have played 133 times. The series was dead even, 57–57, after Wisconsin’s 2014 win. Since then Wisconsin won six and Minnesota five, including the last two: 24–7 in Madison in 2024 and 17–7 in Minneapolis last year.

Why it matters: the winner takes Paul Bunyan’s Axe home. A Minnesota win would also mean three straight over Wisconsin. Neither team is playing for the playoff; this game is the season’s trophy.

If you miss it: “Minnesota owns this rivalry now” will be said, and five games is not ownership. Four or more of five is what a team that wins half the time does about one time in five.

Wisconsin has the fourth-best defense in the country. Minnesota has the 113th-best offense.

Once the schedule is counted, Wisconsin allows 3.9 yards a play, fourth of 138. Minnesota gains 5.5, 113th. The total is 37.5 points, tied for the lowest of the weekend’s 66 major-college games. Expect long drives, punts and field goals.

Why it matters: Minnesota is 6–5 and has been outscored on the season, by a tenth of a point a game. It got to 6–5 by going 4–1 in games decided by eight points or fewer. Close games are mostly coin flips. If this one is close late, that record will not help Minnesota.

If you miss it: a Minnesota field goal drive will look like the offense waking up. Against this defense, a field goal is a good drive.

The halftime check
82of every 100 college teams ahead at the half won this season (597 of 729). Up by one score: 65 (195 of 298).

At halftime, check the score. A one-score lead wins about two times in three.

This season the college team ahead at the half has won 82 times in 100. When the lead is eight points or fewer, it drops to 65. In a game with a 37.5-point total, a one-score halftime is the likeliest thing you will see.

Why it matters: with two defenses this strong, a 10–7 lead at the half is real, but it is a long way from safe.

If you miss it: a 7–3 halftime will feel like a lock in a game this slow. It is about two in three. The live card tracks it: open it on your phone.

Sources. Line, ESPN’s forecast, the weather, season stats and leaders: ESPN’s game page, Wednesday evening. Scores and records: CollegeFootballData, checked against ESPN. Series history: James Howell and CollegeFootballData.

Method. The market’s chance is the moneyline with the bookmaker’s cut removed. ESPN’s chance is its FPI forecast on the game page. Ours comes from SP+ ratings (CollegeFootballData, Nov 25): rating gap plus 2.5 points for home field, through a normal curve with a 16.5-point spread. Playoff chances: we played out the rest of the season 60,000 times, starting each team where the committee put it on Nov. 24 and moving it by what each simulated result does to its record against its schedule. That is a stand-in for the committee, not the committee: it knows nothing of head-to-head, injuries or the eye test. The “with a win / with a loss” numbers split those same seasons by this game’s result. Yards per play is adjusted for the schedule (CollegeFootballData box scores through week 13; rank of 138). Series records: James Howell’s historical scores and CollegeFootballData agree on every game. Halftime rates: every major-college game this season through Nov. 21 with a leader at the half.
