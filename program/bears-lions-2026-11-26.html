Section 1Three things to know

times in 100, from 20,000 simulated seasons

Win, and the Bears become the favorite to win the division. Lose, and it is about one in five.

We played out the rest of the season 20,000 times. Right now the Bears win the NFC North 35 times in 100 and the Lions 45. A Bears win Thursday lifts Chicago to 53; a loss drops it to 19. The only bye in the conference, the No. 1 seed, moves from 14 to 22 with a win and to 6 with a loss.

Why it matters: the division winner hosts a playoff game; a wild card travels. And these two meet again Jan. 3 in Chicago. If they finish tied, head-to-head comes first, and our simulation breaks ties at random, so it actually understates what Thursday is worth.

If you miss it: both teams are likely in the playoffs either way (the Bears 96 in 100 with a win, 84 with a loss). Thursday decides where they play in January, not whether.

The Bears give up yards, not points. The Lions bring the third-best offense in football.

Once the schedule is counted, Chicago allows 5.9 yards a play, 28th of 32. Yet it allows only 19.7 points a game, sixth. Detroit gains 6.2 yards a play, third, and scores 28.8 a game.

Why it matters: one of those Bears numbers is going to give on Thursday. Which one is the real Bears? We tested it this season: yards per play picked 81 of 121 winners from the weeks before, points picked 84. Neither knows much better than the other yet.

If you miss it: a 70-yard Lions drive will feel like a disaster. For this defense it has been normal. Watch what happens inside the 20; that is where the Bears have been winning.

The halftime check
79of every 100 NFL teams ahead at the half won this season (119 of 151). Up by one score: 75 (62 of 83).

At halftime, check the score. A one-score lead is three in four, not a lock.

This season the NFL team ahead at the half has won 79 times in 100. When the lead is eight points or fewer, that drops to 75. Both of these teams have played five one-score games and won four of them.

Why it matters: with a total of 55.5, this could be a 17–14 halftime. One time in four, a lead that size still flips.

If you miss it: a three-point Lions lead at the half will feel like the game. It is roughly the same three-in-four a coin with a heavy thumb gives you. The live card tracks it: open it on your phone.

Gene’s Rule, live

Gene’s rule: you can tell a good team by how fast it answers after the other side scores. Through week 11, after an opponent’s touchdown, the Lions answered with a score 13 times in 24. The Bears answered 6 times in 19, even though they score on 45 in 100 of their other kickoff drives. The league as a whole answers about 38 in 100.

So the Bears are 8–2 and still not a Gene team. Our test found answering is mostly just scoring: teams answer about as often as they score any other time. A Bears offense that scores 45 in 100 otherwise should not stay at 6 of 19. Thursday is a fresh look. Count it with us: each time the Lions score a touchdown, did Chicago’s next drive score?

Method. The market’s chance is the moneyline with the bookmaker’s cut removed. The Odds Board rates every club from the season’s published point spreads (least squares, home edge 1.4 points) and plays out the rest of the season 20,000 times, margins normal with a 13.2-point spread. The “with a win / with a loss” numbers split those same seasons by this game’s result. Ties in the standings are broken at random, not by the league’s rules, so read them as sizes, not decimals. Yards per play is adjusted for the schedule (nflverse play-by-play through week 11); our test this season, calling each week from the weeks before it: yards per play picked 81 of 121 winners, points 84.
