times in 100, from 60,000 simulated seasons

Win, and Oregon makes the playoff about nine times in ten. Lose, and it is out.

We played out the rest of the season 60,000 times from the committee’s Nov. 24 ranking. Oregon makes the 12-team field 68 times in 100 now, 91 with a win, and almost never with a loss. Of every game this weekend, only USC at UCLA moves more playoff probability.

Why it matters: at No. 12, Oregon sits on the edge of the field. A win keeps it there while teams around it lose. A fourth loss drops it behind too many others to recover.

If you miss it: Oregon fans should watch Indiana, Iowa and Texas Tech too: an Oregon win costs each of them a real share of their own playoff chance. Saturday night is a two-way street.

The market likes Oregon more than our ratings do, by eight in 100.

Our ratings come from SP+, which sees Oregon as clearly better but not dominant. The market and ESPN both make Oregon a two-touchdown favorite. Oregon has won four straight, the last three by 13, 24 and 38 points.

Why it matters: when a rating and the market split, the market often knows something about who is playing that a rating cannot. Our ratings know nothing about injuries. We will grade it Sunday either way.

If you miss it: if Washington keeps it close into the fourth quarter, that is not a shock; one forecast in three thought this was more like a three-in-four game.

The halftime check
82of every 100 college teams ahead at the half won this season (597 of 729). Up by one score: 65 (195 of 298).

At halftime, check the score. A one-score lead wins about two times in three.

This season the college team ahead at the half has won 82 times in 100, and 65 when the lead is eight points or fewer. Washington has played seven one-score games and won four of them, including its last two.

Why it matters: a Washington team that is used to close finishes is the wrong team to lead by only a touchdown at the half.

If you miss it: a 14–10 Oregon lead will feel like control. It is about two in three. The live card tracks it: open it on your phone.

Sources. Line, ESPN’s forecast, the weather, season stats and leaders: ESPN’s game page, Wednesday evening. Scores and records: CollegeFootballData, checked against ESPN. Series history: James Howell and CollegeFootballData. Playoff chances: research/cfp-committee/rivalry/ in our repository.

Method. The market’s chance is the moneyline with the bookmaker’s cut removed. ESPN’s chance is its FPI forecast on the game page. Ours comes from SP+ ratings (CollegeFootballData, Nov 25): rating gap plus 2.5 points for home field, through a normal curve with a 16.5-point spread. Playoff chances: we played out the rest of the season 60,000 times, starting each team where the committee put it on Nov. 24 and moving it by what each simulated result does to its record against its schedule. That is a stand-in for the committee, not the committee: it knows nothing of head-to-head, injuries or the eye test. The “with a win / with a loss” numbers split those same seasons by this game’s result. Yards per play is adjusted for the schedule (CollegeFootballData box scores through week 13; rank of 138). Series records: James Howell’s historical scores and CollegeFootballData agree on every game. Halftime rates: every major-college game this season through Nov. 21 with a leader at the half.
