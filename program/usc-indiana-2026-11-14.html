Bottom line: Indiana is a clear favorite, not a lock. Three forecasts agree on about three in four: the betting market (76 in 100), ESPN’s model (78) and our own ratings (73). The catch is the last month. Indiana has been winning by a field goal, not by ten.

Section 1Three things to know

The last month

In regulation over the last four games, Indiana scored 57 points and gave up 58.

The first five games were blowouts: 44 points a game for, 11 against. Then came Nebraska (won 20–17), Ohio State (lost 15–19), Michigan (6–6 after four quarters, won in overtime) and Minnesota (16–16, won in overtime).

Why it matters: the line says Indiana by ten. The last month says Indiana wins close games. Both can be true; a good team in a tight stretch is still a good team. But a ten-point favorite that has not won a game by ten since October 4 deserves a little humility.

If you miss it: a tie game in the fourth quarter will feel like a collapse. For this team it has been a normal Saturday, and it has gone 3–1 in them.

What is riding on it

A loss probably does not end Indiana’s season. It costs the bye.

We played out the rest of the season 60,000 times. When Indiana wins Saturday, it makes the 12-team field about 99 times in 100 and earns a top-four seed, and the week off that comes with it, 64 times in 100. When it loses, it still makes the field about 75 times in 100, but the bye falls to 11.

Why it matters: a top-four seed skips the first round. That is one fewer game to survive, and last year it took four playoff wins to win it all. For USC the game is bigger still: a win lifts its own playoff chance from about 22 in 100 to about 67.

If you miss it: you will hear “win and they’re in” on the broadcast. That is nearly true already. The real prize Saturday is the week off. (Our stand-in for the committee is rough. It reads the résumé, not the eye test, so treat these as sizes, not decimals.)

The halftime check
83of every 100 college teams ahead at the half won this season (514 of 619)

At halftime, look at the score. Then remember Indiana trailed at the half in three of its last four.

This season, the college team ahead at halftime has won 83 times in 100. When the lead is eight points or fewer, that drops to 68. Indiana trailed Nebraska 10–3, Ohio State 10–7 and Minnesota 10–7 at the half, and won two of the three.

Why it matters: an Indiana lead at the half makes this very close to settled. A small USC lead is the one in three that still flips, and it is the script Indiana has been running all month.

If you miss it: a 10–7 halftime deficit will read as trouble. For Indiana lately it has meant a fourth-quarter game, not a loss. The live game card tracks this as it happens: open it on your phone.

Section 2The stat we think matters most

We think. We do not know. Yards per play has been one of the better quick reads of a football team, because every snap counts, not just the ones that score. But it does not know who you played. Seven yards a play against a weak defense is easier than five against a great one. So we adjust every team for its schedule (what that means).

D # is the opponent defense’s rank among 138 major-college teams, adjusted for its own schedule. Dashed line: the average offense. Howard plays in the lower division and is not ranked.

#11

Indiana’s offense, once the schedule is counted: 7.0 yards a play. The slump was mostly the opponents. Three of the last four defenses rank in the top 25.

#2

Indiana’s defense: it allows 3.8 yards a play. Only one team in the country is stingier.

#67

USC’s defense, at 5.2 a play: the weakest Indiana has faced since Rutgers, five games ago.

#24

USC’s offense, at 6.8. It gained 6.8 a play against Ohio State, whose defense ranks 11th. This is a real offense.

What to watch: put the two together and Indiana should gain close to 7 yards a play Saturday, its early-season level, while USC should be held under 5. If Indiana is near 7 at halftime, the October slump was the schedule. If it is stuck near 5 against this defense, something else is wrong.

Why we think so, and why we are not sure: we tested it on this season. Calling each week’s winners from the weeks before it, schedule-adjusted yards per play picked 234 of 359; points picked 228. That is about as good as the scoreboard, not better, and it misses one game in three. Box scores from CollegeFootballData, checked against ESPN.

Last New Year’s Day in the Rose Bowl, over Alabama. Then 56–22 over Oregon, and 27–21 over Miami for the title.

0–4

Indiana against USC, ever. The first was in 1953. The biggest was the Rose Bowl on Jan. 1, 1968: USC 14, Indiana 3. The last was 1982.

So Saturday is a first in a small way. The last time these two met in a game this big, Indiana was the underdog in Pasadena. This time USC comes to Bloomington, and Indiana is the champion.
