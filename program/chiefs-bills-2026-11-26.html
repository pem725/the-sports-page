Section 1Three things to know

Bills: chance of the AFC’s only bye

times in 100, from 20,000 simulated seasons

Win, and Buffalo holds the AFC’s only bye about seven times in ten. Lose, and it falls to four.

We played out the rest of the season 20,000 times. The Bills make the playoffs in essentially all of them, win or lose. What moves is the No. 1 seed: 59 in 100 now, 71 with a win, 40 with a loss. For Kansas City the same seed goes from 11 to 22 with a win, or to 3 with a loss.

Why it matters: only the top seed skips the wild-card round. That is one fewer playoff game to survive, and every AFC game until the Super Bowl played in Buffalo in January.

If you miss it: “the Bills have clinched nothing” will be technically true all night. In practice their place in the playoffs is settled. Thursday is about the week off and home field, and it is a big swing for one game.

Allen v. Mahomes
5–1regular season
0–4playoffs

every meeting since 2020

Josh Allen is 5–1 against Patrick Mahomes in the regular season and 0–4 in the playoffs.

The Bills have won the last five regular-season meetings, three of them in Kansas City. The playoff losses: 38–24 (January 2021), 42–36 in overtime (2022), 27–24 (2024) and 32–29 (2025).

Why it matters: a win Thursday will be sold as the Bills “finally solving” the Chiefs. They have solved them five straight times in November and October. Three of the four January losses were by six points or fewer, and one took overtime. Ten games split 5 and 5 is what two great teams look like; how the wins landed by month is the part that hurts.

If you miss it: you will hear that one result Thursday says something about January. It says about as much as the last five did.

The halftime check

At halftime, check the score. A one-score lead is three in four, not a lock.

This season the NFL team ahead at the half has won 79 times in 100. When the lead is eight points or fewer, that drops to 75. Five of the last six regular-season meetings in this series were decided by ten points or less.

Why it matters: these two offenses both rank in the top four in yards per play (Kansas City 2nd at 6.4, Buffalo 4th at 6.2). A halftime lead in this game is a lead against an offense that can answer.

If you miss it: a 10–7 halftime will feel like the Bills are in control or in trouble, depending on which side has the 10. Either way, one time in four it flips. The live card tracks it: open it on your phone.

Sources. Line, ESPN’s model, the weather forecast, season stats, leaders and injuries: ESPN’s game page, Wednesday morning. Practice report, scores, records and the head-to-head history: nflverse. Halftime rates: every NFL game this season through Nov 23 with a leader at the half.

Method. The market’s chance is the moneyline with the bookmaker’s cut removed. The Odds Board rates every club from the season’s published point spreads (least squares, home edge 1.4 points) and plays out the rest of the season 20,000 times, margins normal with a 13.2-point spread. The “with a win / with a loss” numbers split those same seasons by this game’s result. Ties in the standings are broken at random, not by the league’s rules, so read them as sizes, not decimals. Yards per play is adjusted for the schedule (nflverse play-by-play through week 11); our test this season, calling each week from the weeks before it: yards per play picked 81 of 121 winners, points 84.
