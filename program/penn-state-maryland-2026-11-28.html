Penn State, 2026

Penn State has lost four games by eight points or fewer. A fair coin does that about one time in five.

The four: 24–20 to Wisconsin, 31–23 to USC, 21–18 at Michigan and 25–24 to Rutgers. Three of them were at home. The one close win was 28–25 over Washington. A team that wins half its close games goes 1–4 or worse in five of them about 19 times in 100.

Why it matters: close games are where luck lives. Penn State’s other six games were decided by 14 points or more, and it won five. 6–5 undersells this team, and the market price on Saturday says so.

If you miss it: you will hear that Penn State “can’t win close games,” as if it were a trait. Over a season or two, records in close games mostly drift back to even. Watch the fourth quarter as a coin flip, not a character test.

Maryland has beaten Penn State three times in 48 tries.

Maryland won in 1961, 2014 and 2020. The 2014 game was 20–19 in State College; the 2020 game was 35–19, also there. Penn State has won the last four, the most recent 44–7 in 2024.

Why it matters: history is not a forecast; this season is. Maryland is 4–7, has allowed 31 points a game, and lost its last game 35–21 to USC. But it beat Wisconsin by three, and Wisconsin beat Penn State.

If you miss it: “Penn State owns Maryland” is true about the past. Saturday’s seven-in-ten comes from 2026, not from 1961.

The halftime check
82of every 100 college teams ahead at the half won this season (597 of 729). Up by one score: 65 (195 of 298).

At halftime, check the score. A one-score lead wins about two times in three.

This season the college team ahead at the half has won 82 times in 100. When the lead is eight points or fewer, it drops to 65. For Penn State this year, one-score games have gone the wrong way four times in five.

Why it matters: if Penn State leads by two scores at the half, relax. If it is a one-score game, you are watching the part of the season that has gone badly.

If you miss it: a seven-point halftime lead will feel safe. It is about two in three. The live card tracks it: open it on your phone.

Sources. Line, ESPN’s forecast, the weather, season stats and leaders: ESPN’s game page, Wednesday evening. Scores and records: CollegeFootballData, checked against ESPN. Series history: James Howell and CollegeFootballData.

Method. The market’s chance is the moneyline with the bookmaker’s cut removed. ESPN’s chance is its FPI forecast on the game page. Ours comes from SP+ ratings (CollegeFootballData, Nov 25): rating gap plus 2.5 points for home field, through a normal curve with a 16.5-point spread. Playoff chances: we played out the rest of the season 60,000 times, starting each team where the committee put it on Nov. 24 and moving it by what each simulated result does to its record against its schedule. That is a stand-in for the committee, not the committee: it knows nothing of head-to-head, injuries or the eye test. The “with a win / with a loss” numbers split those same seasons by this game’s result. Yards per play is adjusted for the schedule (CollegeFootballData box scores through week 13; rank of 138). Series records: James Howell’s historical scores and CollegeFootballData agree on every game. Halftime rates: every major-college game this season through Nov. 21 with a leader at the half.
