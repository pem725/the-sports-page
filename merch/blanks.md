# Choosing the blank — options against "size, comfort, low price"

The garment in `README.md` was chosen on one criterion: **colour**. Gildan
Softstyle in Natural sits at ΔE 3.5 from the site's `--aged #e0d8c5`, so the
shirt is not coordinated with the newspaper, it *is* the newspaper.

That is still a good reason. But it was never tested against a different brief —
the one actually stated: *"I shop for size and comfort, and low price."* This
note works that brief.

## The problem with the current pick, on this brief

**Gildan Softstyle is the slimmest of the cheap shirts.** It is 4.5 oz and cut
with a taper. That is exactly why it feels "soft" on a rack and exactly why it is
the wrong shirt for someone who shops for room. Anyone between sizes sizes up in
this blank, and the whole size run then runs long in the body.

It is also, notably, *not* cheaper than the roomy alternatives by much. It beats
Ultra Cotton by 30 cents and Port & Company by $1.50.

## Three options

### Option A — Stay. Gildan Softstyle, Natural. $11.65
Cheapest, and the only one whose colour match is already measured and whose art
is already built for it. Choose this if the ΔE 3.5 match matters more than fit.
**Cost of changing nothing: zero.**

### Option B — Hanes Beefy-T, Natural. $14.40  ← recommended
6.0 oz, genuinely roomy, the best-reviewed shirt on the list at 4.7, and built to
survive a hundred washes — which is what "bought at an outlet and still in the
closet" actually means. **$2.75/shirt over the current pick**, or $69 on a run of
25. It carries a Natural colourway, so the design intent survives.

This is the closest match to the stated brief: size, comfort, low price, in that
order.

### Option C — Comfort Colors 1717, Ivory. $16.95
The best-feeling shirt here and the one people keep. Garment-dyed, so it arrives
already soft rather than softening later.

Two real costs. It is **$5.30/shirt over the current pick**, and garment dyeing
means **colour varies between batches** — which directly undermines the ΔE
argument that picked the original. You would be trading a measured colour match
for a better hand. That may well be worth it; just make it knowingly.

## What does NOT transfer if the blank changes

Read this before ordering anything other than Option A.

1. **The ΔE match must be re-measured.** The 3.5 figure is specific to Gildan
   Softstyle "Natural." Every mill's "Natural" is a different colour. Pull
   physical swatches and measure — do not trust a website render, and do not
   assume the name means the colour.
2. **The halo hex must change with the garment.** `README.md` is explicit: the
   halo where the curve crosses the S is a *painted stroke in the ground colour*,
   not an absence of ink. On DTG it prints literally, so a cream halo on a
   different natural lays down a visible ring. New garment means a new
   `-natural` variant of the art.
3. **Youth and extended sizes differ by blank.** Softstyle Natural runs YS–3XL
   with no XS and nothing above 3XL. Confirm the run on whichever blank wins
   before promising anyone a size.

## Suggested next step

Order **one blank sample each of B and C in their natural colourway**, put them
next to a printed page of the site, and photograph all three together under
daylight. That settles the colour question with a measurement instead of an
argument, and settles the fit question by putting the shirt on.

Sample cost is a few dollars. A wrong run of 25 is not.
