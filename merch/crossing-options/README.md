# Where the bell curve crosses the S — four options

Made 2026-09-24, while Order #3480 (EmbroideryINC, 12 Mercer+Mettle MM1000 polos)
sat waiting on proof approval. The editor's objection: *"I guess the knockout halo
is the part that I don't like."*

**Look at `COMPARE.png` first.** `COMPARE-crossing-detail.png` is the same thing
cropped tight on the crossing, which is where the whole argument lives.

## READ THIS FIRST — the halo was never knocked out

The two Signal photographs in this folder are the physical sew-outs, and at
magnification they settle the question:

**On the white sew-out, the halo is stitched in WHITE THREAD.** It is not open
fabric. The backing is a warm cream felt with visible loose fibers; the halo is a
cold, bright, raised satin stitch with regular stitch direction and obvious sheen.
They are plainly different materials. The written instruction was explicit —
*"a knockout to open garment fabric, not an additional thread color... do not
stitch or add a background"* — and it was not followed.

**On the dark sew-out the roles are swapped.** The halo is white, the same color
as the badge, so it disappears into the S — and the curve was stitched in **royal
blue** to stay visible. Two thread colors again, where one was specified.

**This matters more than it sounds.** The editor's objection was *"the knockout
halo is the part that I don't like"* — but he has never seen a knockout halo. He
is looking at a hard white stripe stitched through his logo, which reads as a
third element and produces the racing-stripe effect. An actual knockout is
recessive: garment fabric showing through, no sheen, no raised edge. It would look
materially calmer than what is in these photographs.

**So decide in this order.** First ask whether the design was ever executed. Only
then ask whether the design is wrong. On this evidence option A has not yet been
seen by anybody.

Worth noting too: the dark version, its accident and all, is the better looking of
the two, and for the reason option `D` exists — it separates the curve by color
rather than by a gap.

## What the "knockout halo" actually is

It is not a knockout. The curve is drawn **twice**, one stroke on top of the other:

```
stroke="#f9f2de"  width 5.88   <- cream, underneath
stroke="#051954"  width 2.80   <- navy, on top
```

The cream stroke is painted *in the garment color*, standing 1.54 mm proud on each
side. On a cream polo it passes for open fabric. That is why the crossing reads as
**navy, cream, navy, cream** — four stripes stacked — and why it looks busy.

## The options

| file | what it is | trade |

| `A-current-halo-1.54mm.svg` | what they stitched | complete curve, four stripes at the crossing |
| `B-halo-0.70mm.svg` | halo halved | complete curve, most of the stripe effect gone |
| `E-void-3.00mm.svg` | curve as a single void | calm crossing; **the tails vanish off the ink** |
| `D-contrast-curve.svg` | no gap, curve in a second color | complete curve, clean crossing, **two threads** |

## The option that does not exist

Curve and S the same navy, touching, no separation at all: **the curve disappears
into the S entirely.** Rendered and discarded. That is the reason the halo was
there in the first place, and it is worth knowing before asking for it to be
removed.

## One observation about the dark sew-out

The navy-polo sew-out came back with the curve in navy thread against a cream
badge — which does not match the written instruction of *one cream thread*. But
separating the curve by **color** instead of by a **gap** needs no halo, keeps the
whole curve, and is arguably the best-looking result of the four. `D` is that idea
drawn deliberately. If it is the one, the fix is not "request changes" but "do
that on both garments."

## If a change is requested

Give the digitizer the numbers, not adjectives. Every file here is 82 mm wide,
real size, and the only thing that varies between them is the two stroke widths
and colors listed above.
